About

I am a third-year PhD student at the AGH Doctoral School, working on monitoring, modelling and mitigation of drainage-induced compaction of rock mass. My main study area is the Vietnamese Mekong Delta, where groundwater extraction drives land subsidence that outpaces sea-level rise many times over. I also work on post-mining areas in Poland, where mine flooding causes uplift and sinkholes.

The work combines three sources of evidence. Satellite geodesy (GRACE/GRACE-FO gravimetry and InSAR from the European Ground Motion Service) shows what is happening. Numerical hydrogeological models (NATSUB3D, iMOD) explain why. Machine learning connects the two by downscaling coarse GRACE groundwater signals to scales where subsidence and water management decisions happen.

Alongside research I write for Geoawesome, a geospatial media publication, where I produce articles and interactive maps on Earth observation and GIS.

Research

Four lines of work, from model to satellite.

Numerical modelling · Mekong Delta

Coupling shallow and deep compaction

Delta subsidence has two main components: natural compaction of young Holocene sediments and drainage-induced compaction from groundwater pumping. They are usually modelled separately, which misestimates both the components and the total.

During a three-month internship at the University of Padua I developed a coupling between NATSUB3D (shallow, sedimentation-driven compaction) and iMOD (groundwater flow and deep aquifer compaction), with an exchange of hydrological responses between the two models. On a theoretical delta setup, deep pumping drives extra downward flow that forces shallow compaction.

0.017 → 0.092 mmax. shallow compaction

Satellite gravimetry · machine learning

Downscaling GRACE groundwater storage

GRACE measures changes in total water storage worldwide, but its footprint spans hundreds of kilometres. That is too coarse for the scale where groundwater stress causes land subsidence.

The pipeline subtracts soil moisture, snow, canopy water (ERA5-Land, GLDAS) and an independent surface water model from GRACE TWS, then trains a Random Forest per month on a 96-variable predictor stack (terrain, soil, land cover, surface water occurrence, vegetation, climate). Predictions at 0.01° are residual-corrected so they reproduce the original GRACE signal exactly, and are validated against a calibrated groundwater model of the delta, wells and the G3P product.

InSAR · open-source tooling

MoWaTracK: hydrologically driven ground motion in Poland

MoWaTracK is a Python tool that turns EGMS Level 2b (calibrated) and Level 3 (ortho) data into vertical land motion for a chosen area. It estimates velocities with linear regression, Gaussian weighting or LOESS, decomposes ascending and descending line-of-sight into vertical and horizontal components, and classifies each point's trajectory.

Tested on three Polish sites: Siersza Mine, Greater Poland and the Vistula Delta. A key finding: annual-window fitting captures nonlinear deformation that a single whole-period velocity hides.

Post-mining hazards · Siersza

Detecting sinkholes with InSAR and airborne laser scanning

In the flooded, abandoned Siersza coal mine, InSAR captures regional uplift and residual subsidence after closure, while ALS reveals point-like sinkhole signals that radar misses. I compared raster differencing, low-pass filtering and M3C2 on ALS data, using the Polish Geological Institute sinkhole database as ground truth and 163 levelling benchmarks (2019–2022) for validation.
